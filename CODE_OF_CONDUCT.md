# Code of Conduct

## The Nexment Project

The Nexment Project is built by and for people who believe that software is better when people work together with respect.

Our community should be a place where developers, contributors, maintainers, learners, and newcomers can participate without fear of humiliation, harassment, abuse, or disrespect.

We expect everyone to treat each other as human beings first.

## Our Principles

### Respect Everyone

Treat every person with dignity and respect, regardless of their experience, skill level, background, opinions, or role in the project.

A beginner deserves the same basic respect as an experienced developer.

Do not judge someone's intelligence, ability, education, or worth based on their contribution.

### Criticize Ideas, Not People

Technical disagreement is normal and encouraged.

We may disagree strongly about architecture, code, design, implementation, or project direction. That is part of building good software.

However:

- Criticize the idea, not the person.
- Explain what is wrong and why.
- Suggest better alternatives when possible.
- Do not turn technical disagreement into personal attacks.
- Do not use someone's mistakes to humiliate them.

Being wrong about something is not a reason to disrespect someone.

### Protect Each Other's Dignity

No contribution, question, mistake, or disagreement should be used to embarrass or humiliate another person.

Do not deliberately attack someone's confidence, intelligence, abilities, reputation, or character.

Do not mock beginners for asking basic questions.

Do not make someone feel unwelcome because they are still learning.

Everyone starts somewhere.

## Zero Tolerance for Abuse

The Nexment Project does not tolerate abuse or harassment.

This includes, but is not limited to:

- Personal attacks
- Threats
- Harassment
- Bullying
- Intimidation
- Hate speech
- Discriminatory behavior
- Sexual harassment
- Deliberate humiliation
- Repeated unwanted contact
- Doxxing or exposing private information
- Blackmail or coercion
- Extortion
- Attempts to damage someone's reputation through harassment
- Abuse of project authority or permissions
- Retaliation against someone for raising a concern

Jokes are not an excuse for harmful behavior.

Intent does not automatically remove the impact of harmful behavior.

## Brotherhood and Collaboration

Nexment should feel like a community where people help each other grow.

We encourage contributors to:

- Help newcomers understand the project.
- Share knowledge openly.
- Give constructive feedback.
- Recognize good work.
- Ask for help when something is unclear.
- Help others without looking down on them.
- Give people reasonable time to respond.
- Assume good intentions while still addressing harmful behavior.
- Work together rather than against each other.

Nobody is expected to know everything.

Asking for help is a strength, not a weakness.

## Discussions and Asking for Help

If you are stuck, confused, or unsure about something, use the project's discussion or appropriate community channel and ask for help.

When asking for help:

- Clearly explain the problem.
- Include relevant context.
- Share useful error messages or examples.
- Be patient while others investigate.
- Respect people who volunteer their time to help.

When helping someone:

- Explain rather than insult.
- Be patient with beginners.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon when it creates confusion.
- Correct mistakes respectfully.
- Do not make someone feel stupid for not knowing something.

For general support or situations where you are uncomfortable asking publicly, you can contact:

**support@nexment.in**

## Professional Communication

Keep communication constructive and appropriate.

Before posting a message, comment, issue, or pull request, consider whether it helps move the project forward.

Avoid:

- Aggressive language
- Insults
- Personal arguments
- Provocation
- Unnecessary hostility
- Repeatedly demanding responses
- Derailing technical discussions
- Bringing unrelated personal conflicts into the project

You can be direct without being disrespectful.

You can disagree without being hostile.

## Pull Requests and Code Reviews

Code review exists to improve the project, not to judge the person who wrote the code.

Reviewers should:

- Explain technical concerns clearly.
- Focus on the code and its impact.
- Provide actionable feedback.
- Recognize good solutions.
- Avoid insulting or dismissive language.

Contributors should:

- Be open to reasonable feedback.
- Explain their decisions when necessary.
- Avoid taking technical criticism as a personal attack.
- Ask questions when feedback is unclear.

Maintainers and contributors may disagree. The discussion should remain professional.

## Privacy

Respect the privacy of everyone in the community.

Do not publish or share another person's private information without their permission.

This includes personal contact information, private conversations, private accounts, credentials, or other information that was not intended to be public.

Never use private information to threaten, pressure, embarrass, blackmail, or harm another person.

## Security

Security is a shared responsibility. If you discover a security vulnerability, please report it responsibly rather than publicly disclosing it.

For security vulnerabilities, suspicious activity, exposed credentials, or other security-related concerns, contact:

**admin@nexment.in**

Please do not use public issues, discussions, pull requests, or other public channels to report security vulnerabilities.

When reporting a vulnerability, include enough information for the maintainers to understand and reproduce the issue, while avoiding unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.

We appreciate responsible security research and will treat good-faith reports with respect.

## Handling Conflicts

Disagreements should first be handled calmly and directly when it is safe and appropriate to do so.

If a discussion becomes unproductive:

1. Stop the personal argument.
2. Return to the technical or community issue.
3. Ask a maintainer or moderator for assistance when necessary.
4. Move the discussion to an appropriate private channel if requested.

Do not retaliate against someone for reporting a problem or asking for help.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take appropriate action when this Code of Conduct is violated.

Depending on the situation, actions may include:

- Asking participants to stop inappropriate behavior.
- Removing harmful content.
- Closing or moderating discussions.
- Temporarily restricting participation.
- Removing access to project resources.
- Permanent removal from the community in serious or repeated cases.

Serious behavior such as threats, blackmail, coercion, harassment, or deliberate attempts to harm others may result in immediate action.

Enforcement decisions should focus on protecting the community, maintaining a respectful environment, and preventing further harm.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, please report it to the project maintainers or contact:

**support@nexment.in**

When reporting an issue, provide as much relevant context as you safely can.

Reports will be handled with care and should not be used as a weapon against others.

False reports made intentionally to harass, threaten, or harm another person are themselves a violation of this Code of Conduct.

## No Retaliation

Nobody should be punished, threatened, blackmailed, or harassed for:

- Asking for help.
- Reporting misconduct.
- Participating in an investigation.
- Providing honest feedback.
- Disagreeing respectfully.
- Making a good-faith contribution.

Retaliation is not acceptable.

## A Shared Responsibility

A healthy open-source community is not created by maintainers alone.

Every contributor has a responsibility to help maintain a respectful environment.

If you see someone struggling, help them.

If you disagree, explain your position.

If you make a mistake, learn from it.

If someone helps you, appreciate their effort.

If something feels wrong, speak up.

We build better software when we build each other up rather than tear each other down.

## Final Principle

**Brotherhood over ego.  
Respect over hostility.  
Learning over humiliation.  
Collaboration over conflict.  
People before code.**

The Nexment Project welcomes people who want to learn, build, contribute, and help others do the same.
